Colour-Blind Checkout

Team-built 3D first-person puzzle game about finding groceries when colour can't be trusted

View Project

Overview

Colour-Blind Checkout is a light-hearted 3D first-person exploration and puzzle game set inside a mundane, poorly organised grocery store. The player is handed a shopping list of visually described items, such as "red strawberry yoghurt", and has to find and collect them within a time limit. They quickly discover that in this world, they are colour blind.

With colour no longer reliable, players identify products through silhouette, packaging pattern, poorly written labels, lighting, shelf placement and aisle context. At checkout, the collected items are revealed by name and the player receives accuracy-based feedback, finding out whether their reading of the shelves matched the store's.

The game was a university team project built for a QAGOMA brief and inspired by Nick Cave's Heard (2012), exploring how colour and perception influence recognition and interpretation. It was invited to and showcased at the IT & Games Capstone Showcase at QUT.

My Role: Core Gameplay Programmer

I was the core gameplay programmer on the team, responsible for the systems the player touches every moment of a run, and for the data structures the rest of the game is built on.

  • Player movement and first-person camera controls
  • Item interaction system for selecting, taking and returning products
  • Item inspection view for examining products up close
  • Basket inventory with drag-to-arrange, drag-out-to-return and gamepad support
  • Core data structures defining products, categories and shopping list entries

Core Loop

Because colour can't be relied on, every pick is a small act of close looking: the player compares the cues that remain and commits to a best guess.

  1. Read the next item on the grocery list
  2. Search the store, comparing shape, pattern, text, placement and context
  3. Inspect a candidate up close, then take it or return it
  4. Repeat until the list is attempted, then reveal the basket at checkout

Key Systems

Player Movement & Camera

I built the first-person controller and camera that carry the player through the store. Movement and look controls had to feel relaxed and readable for a casual gallery audience, while still letting players line up shelves and products precisely enough to compare them.

Item Interaction & Inspection

Clicking a product brings it into an inspection view where the player can examine it up close before committing. Large Return and Take buttons keep the choice clear and touch-friendly, which mattered because the game targets touch-screen play for younger audiences.

Inspection is where the core idea lands: with colour stripped back, the player studies silhouette, surface detail and packaging to decide whether the product matches their list.

Basket Inventory

I implemented the basket inventory, which shows the player's collected groceries with their category and the current basket load. Items can be dragged to rearrange or dragged outside the grid to return them to the store, and the same actions are mapped to gamepad buttons.

Product Data Structures

I defined the core structures that describe products: their identity, category and the information the shopping list, inspection view, inventory and checkout reveal all read from. Keeping that in one shared definition gave the rest of the team a consistent base to build store content on.

Outcome

Colour-Blind Checkout turns an everyday errand into a playful exploration of perception. By making colour unreliable, it pushes players to notice the shape, pattern, text and context they normally overlook, meeting the brief's goal of showing gallery visitors how subjective colour and perception can be.
