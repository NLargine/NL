Three Beacons

A third-person sci-fi shooter in Unreal Engine 5.9, made solo in four days for the Epic MegaJam 2026 by directing a crew of AI agents.

View Project

Overview

Trailer, cut from gameplay recordings of the jam build.

Nine years ago the beacons that linked every colony world went dark. The fleet's last mind, the Director, wants to relight them and film it, because that footage will be the first thing the silent worlds see. It prints one agent three times, tells each she has scene one, and leaves one thing out of the briefing.

Three Beacons is my solo entry for the Epic MegaJam 2026, on the theme Director's Cut. You fight across four scenes (a rain-soaked megacity, a canyon outpost, a drowned city and the Meridian station), light each world's dead beacon and hold it while it charges, then face the gunship that caused the blackout. It runs 20 to 30 minutes, with a fully voiced Director.

The jam was the first built around agentic development, with a preview of UE 5.9 and an MCP that lets AI agents work inside the editor. So I took the theme literally: I was the director. A coordinator agent split the work across parallel Claude Code sessions for levels, guns, effects, sound, cinematics and packaging. I set the vision, playtested every build and decided what made the cut.

4 days · 1 developer · a crew of AI agents. Four hand-built worlds, three playable takes, a gunship boss, voiced cinematics and a Director who never stops rolling.

What's In It

Three Takes, Four Scenes

Each scene gives you a different take of the same agent: Polar with twin pistols in the city, Bulwark with a machine gun in the canyon, and Hazard with a sniper and sidearm in the drowned city. Each has her own skills and an orbital strike. On the Meridian all three meet in one frame for the finale.

Every world is its own mood: neon rain, red dust, a city swallowed by the sea, and a station hanging between planets. The levels are built in Blender and Substance 3D Painter and lit with Lumen hardware ray tracing, Nanite and virtual shadow maps.

Shooting and Movement

Free aim on a shoulder camera, recoil that kicks the arm that fired, spread, reloads, headshots, hit-stop and ragdoll deaths thrown along the killing shot.

Between fights you dodge, blink and hover, fly across rifts between towers, launch off glide pads and grind rails across the flooded bay, so each level plays as a route rather than a single arena.

The Gunship

The final boss is a multi-phase gunship with armour plates to break, a sweeping laser, missiles and bullet-hell patterns, fought by all three takes on the Meridian with planets hanging in the sky behind it.

Through the Director's Lens

The theme runs through the presentation. The HUD is a viewfinder with REC, timecode, scene and take. A clapperboard slates every fight and grades every mission, and a death is "CUT! Take 2".

The final kill of each fight plays a kill cam that prints a still of your best moment, and the ending collects those stills as "The Director's Cut". Lost takes hidden in each level are the Director's outtakes.

Directing the Crew

  • The vision from day one: a shooter with the fidelity Unreal is known for, not a weekend tech demo
  • A coordinator agent ran parallel Claude Code sessions, one each for levels, guns, effects, sound, cinematics and packaging
  • Agents worked live inside Unreal through its MCP, and in Blender and Substance Painter through theirs
  • Every change came back with automated test runs and captured screenshots, so I could judge it at a glance
  • I playtested every build, and my notes drove each round of fixes
  • Like any director, I cut what didn't earn its place, including a whole boss and five enemy types
