Ghost Of Us

Narrative adventure with party systems, quests, and reactive world state

View Project

Overview

Ghost Of Us is a narrative adventure built in Unity that combines character-driven storytelling with exploration, inventory, combat support systems, and reactive environmental states. The project expands on ideas from earlier work by aiming for a more cohesive, believable world rather than a sequence of disconnected mechanics.

Its strongest portfolio value is the number of interconnected systems it brings together: party management, quest progression, inventory, dialogue, interactables, and persistent state changes across scenes.

Key Systems

Runtime Lighting & Environmental Reactivity

Runtime states track the aquarium lighting sequence and other environmental conditions, allowing the player to interact with the world and see those interactions reflected back in the scene.

Inventory & Party Equipment

The inventory system supports consumables, key items, equippables such as weapons and flashlights, and placeable traps. It includes persistence, player-controlled rearrangement, and party-aware item transfers.

Quest System & Quest Log

The quest system supports long-form quests with multiple steps, requirements, rewards, and world manipulation. In Ghost Of Us, quest progress can unlock access, change the environment, and alter NPC dialogue to reflect the player's actions.

Runtime State Tracking

The game tracks persistent runtime states across scenes. That system is used for adventure completion, destructible object behaviour, time-based respawns, and continuity checks such as whether the player has already triggered certain world events.

Puzzles & Minigames

The project includes puzzle interactions and bespoke minigames, such as clue-based problem solving in the aquarium, to diversify the pacing of exploration.

Party System

The player can switch between party members, have allies follow in the world, receive combat support, and engage with revive logic that works for both the player and AI companions. Different priorities and relationship-driven behaviour help the system feel less static.

Dialogue System

The dialogue system supports sprite and Live2D characters along with animation changes, expressions, player choices, audio cues, and hooks into runtime state logic. That made it a central piece of both the narrative and systems side of the project.

Interactable Environments

Interactable objects throughout the game help sell the world as a space the player can engage with rather than just move through.

Asset Creation

I created sprites, illustrations, game assets, UI, and Spine2D playable characters to support the tone and readability of the experience.

Shop System

The shop system gives the player a grounded way to buy items and reinforces the feeling that the world has functional spaces and routines.

Other Supporting Features

  • Dynamic audio: Sound effects adjust based on player proximity.
  • 4 distinct environments: Each with its own adventure flow and interactions.
  • Equippable items: Flashlights, weapons, and other tools with their own parameters.
  • Interactive minigames: Including a haunted house and shooting range.
  • NPC management: Support for persistent characters and reactive world dialogue.
  • Core systems: Save/load and ability systems for longer-form progression.
  • Accessibility: Adjustable text speed and volume controls.

Design Decisions

One of the key goals for Ghost Of Us was making the world feel reactive rather than static. Systems such as quests, lighting states, dialogue progression, party behaviour, and interactables were all built to reinforce the idea that the player's actions have tangible effects on the space around them.

The result is a project that reads less like a single-mechanic prototype and more like a systems-rich narrative adventure where exploration, story, and progression support each other.
