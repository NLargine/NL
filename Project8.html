Top-down shooter and base defence hybrid built solo in Unity

View Project

Overview

Annie's Last Stand is a top-down shoot 'em up fused with real-time base defence, built solo in Unity in just under three weeks as a first-cycle university project (IGB100, QUT). Players take control of Annie, a lone elite operative defending the world's last nuclear silo against relentless waves of autonomous mechs.

Defeated enemies drop Scrap, which can be spent at an in-world shop on weapon upgrades, deployable turrets, and wall repairs, creating a constant tension between immediate survival and long-term investment. Enemies never stop coming, so the player is always balancing positioning, target priority, and base maintenance.

The project aimed to merge SHMUP intensity with a more strategic defensive loop. It pushed well beyond the original assignment scope and received a High Distinction.

Key Systems

Combat & Projectile System

The player uses 360-degree free-aim combat with mouse tracking to control aim direction. A bullet pooling manager supports high projectile counts without costly runtime instantiation, while multiple weapons expose different fire rates, damage profiles, and spread patterns that can all be upgraded through the scrap economy.

Enemy AI & Spawning

Rather than rely on discrete rounds, enemies spawn continuously from drop ships at the edges of the map to maintain pressure. Each enemy type has a different tactical role: ground units pathfind toward objectives, aerial units ignore wall layouts entirely, and heavier units focus on structural damage.

Scrap Economy & Shop System

Enemies drop Scrap on death, which is collected by proximity and spent at an in-world shop near the generators. Players use it on turrets, ammo, healing, and weapon upgrades, creating a real risk-reward loop between pushing forward for resources and falling back to reinforce the defence line.

Multi-Point Generator Defence

Four generators simultaneously contribute passive progress toward launch. Each one can be independently damaged or destroyed, so the player is constantly choosing where to stand, where to spend scrap, and how to place walls and turrets when no single defensive position can protect every objective at once.

Enemy Roster

Seven enemy types plus a support unit create layered threat management across both ground and aerial dimensions.

Design Decisions

Player Experience Goals

Three core goals shaped the project: urgency, tension, and strategic mastery. The game needed to feel hectic and pressuring in the moment, but still reward deliberate upgrades, movement decisions, and defensive planning.

Key Revisions

The initial concept proposed a squad-based system with AI-controlled allies. Feedback showed that managing multiple agents would dilute the core SHMUP emphasis on precise movement and dodging, so the design pivoted to a single-player lone-operative structure with strategic depth coming from deployable walls and turrets.

A second revision replaced the original wave-based spawning structure with continuous pressure after feedback showed that downtime between waves reduced urgency and undermined the intended last-stand fantasy.
