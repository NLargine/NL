War On Water

Team-built top-down survivor-like with gameplay systems, upgrades, and UI integration

View Project

Overview

War On Water is a short-session survival shooter built in Unity where the player controls a fire elemental holding out against escalating waves of water enemies. A full run lasts around five minutes, with the core loop centered on movement, positioning, and fast upgrade choices under pressure.

This was a team project and my main contribution was driving the game into a cohesive, playable whole. I led development across gameplay programming, systems design, UI implementation, and technical integration so the combat loop, progression, enemy pressure, and feedback all worked together cleanly.

Key Systems

Wave Director & Enemy Pressure

I built the core survival loop around escalating waves that scale enemy count, speed, and health over the course of a run. Multiple enemy types, including melee, ranged, explosive, and boss encounters with phase-based behaviour, create different kinds of pressure rather than just bigger numbers.

Modular Ability & Upgrade System

The upgrade structure uses ScriptableObjects and a weighted random "choose 1 of 3" card system to keep progression readable while still allowing variation between runs. I implemented nine active and passive abilities with multi-tier upgrades and designed the system to be easy to extend and rebalance.

Performance for High Enemy Density

Because the game's readability depends on maintaining pressure without technical slowdown, I implemented custom object pooling for enemies, projectiles, and effects. That reduced runtime instantiation overhead and let the game sustain denser combat scenarios more reliably.

UI, Feedback & VFX Integration

I developed the full gameplay UI flow, including HUD, level-up cards, pause, death, and victory states, then paired that with hit effects, particles, trails, and shader work to improve combat readability. That integration work was important because the game needed to stay legible as enemy counts and overlapping effects increased.

Challenge & Problem Solving

A major challenge was preserving game feel and readability under high enemy density. As the run scales up, large numbers of enemies and effects can quickly turn into visual noise, especially in a top-down survivor-like where the player needs to read danger, impact, and safe movement space at a glance.

To solve that, I introduced clearer particle and hit feedback, tuned ability visuals to better communicate impact and area coverage, and balanced scaling so later waves still felt threatening without overwhelming the screen. The result was a more readable and satisfying late-game combat state, which was one of the most important quality improvements on the project.
