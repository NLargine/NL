Lost Kingdoms

Systems-heavy RPG prototype focused on expeditions, kingdom building, and progression

View Project

Overview

Lost Kingdoms is an ambitious work-in-progress RPG prototype built around kingdom building, expedition design, party progression, and long-form system interplay. The project leans into breadth: the player develops a home base, ventures into procedural mission spaces, gathers materials, unlocks stronger regions, and gradually takes on more dangerous enemy factions.

One of the strongest portfolio signals here is how many connected gameplay layers are already in motion: procedural expeditions, base upgrading, resource loops, party management, ability progression, and combat encounters that demand more than simple stat checks.

Key Systems

Trap and Item Placement

The trap and placement system allows items, traps, and furniture to be placed into scenes while retaining their stored position and persistent state. That makes player construction and preparation feel grounded rather than temporary.

Object Pooling and Optimization

Because the game can contain large numbers of placeable items, it uses object pooling to reuse existing objects instead of instantiating everything at runtime. Similar objects can also be merged when untouched, reducing clutter and helping the world scale more efficiently.

Boss Encounters

Boss fights are built around more than raw damage. Encounters can include mechanics like stunning a boss with traps, using pillars to create environmental knockdowns, and handling heal retreats and phase-style move variation.

Ability and Spell System

The ability system supports offensive, support, and healing skills for both the player and party members. Each ability can progress individually, with multiple skill sets encouraging build variety and flexible combat roles.

Party System

The party system allows the player to switch between characters, receive combat support from followers, and interact with downed and revival states. Companion behaviour also varies based on internal priorities, which helps the system feel less rigid.

Design Decisions

Lost Kingdoms is less about a single standout mechanic and more about how multiple systems support a longer-term fantasy of rebuilding, exploring, and steadily becoming more capable. Expeditions, material gathering, ability progression, and kingdom upgrades were designed to feed each other rather than exist as disconnected features.

That makes it a useful portfolio piece for showing large-system thinking: the challenge is not just building each mechanic, but making them all point in the same direction.

Training Grounds

The training grounds act as an arena where the player can test weapons, abilities, and mission variations ranging from base defence to night survival.

Shader Showcase

This shader showcase combines stochastic texturing for less repetitive lava surfaces with 2D shader graph motion controls. It also demonstrates full directional aiming for Spine2D characters, allowing attacks and animation to line up across all aim directions.

Enemy Camp Encounter

This dev log tests an early raid against the Boar Camp. The player and recruited party members use crafted and looted gear to navigate the encounter and destroy the enemy position.

Farming and Neutral Creatures

As part of the kingdom-building loop, neutral animals can be farmed for resources such as milk, eggs, leather, and meat, feeding back into the wider economy and crafting progression.

Vaults and Clue Solving

By searching for clues throughout the game, the player can find rare skill books and trigger a vault-solving minigame to unlock them.

Kingdom Building, Storage, and Crafting

The kingdom-building system supports persistent storage, HQ upgrades, production structures, research bonuses, and defensive buildings. Upgrading the HQ and supporting structures expands crafting, improves stats, and opens up stronger progression options across the rest of the game.

Kingdom Building Follow-up

A second showcase covering more of the base-building loop and progression flow.

Day and Night System

The day-night system runs on a time model that affects lighting and broader world presentation. Players can also sleep to skip forward and manage risk or pacing.

Dungeons and Harvesting

Harvesting tools can be given to party members and are used across multiple resource types. Different tiers of resources require stronger tools, tying gathering progression directly into crafting, exploration, and combat readiness.

RTS-Style Quest

This quest line takes inspiration from Age of Empires and MOBA lane structures. The player constructs defences, intercepts supply lines, manages two active fronts, and works toward destroying the enemy command centre while keeping their own base intact.

Visual Novel Shader Work

The visual novel presentation layer uses shaders to transition sprites, backgrounds, and cinematic moments more smoothly than simple hard cuts.
