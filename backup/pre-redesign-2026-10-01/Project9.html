War On Water

Team-built top-down survivor-like with elemental abilities, upgrade systems, enemy escalation, and UI integration

View Project

Overview

War On Water is a short-session survival shooter built in Unity where the player controls a fire elemental holding out against escalating waves of water enemies. A full run lasts around five minutes, with the core loop centered on movement, positioning, and fast upgrade choices under pressure.

This was a team project and my main contribution was driving the game into a cohesive, playable whole. I led development across gameplay programming, systems design, UI implementation, technical integration, ability progression, and combat readability so the progression loop, enemy pressure, and visual feedback all worked together cleanly.

Key Systems

Wave Director & Enemy Pressure

I built the core survival loop around escalating waves that scale enemy count, speed, and health over the course of a run. Multiple enemy types, including melee, ranged, explosive, boss encounters, and Tier 2 glowing enemies, create different kinds of pressure rather than just bigger numbers.

Modular Ability & Upgrade System

The upgrade structure uses ScriptableObjects and a weighted random "choose 1 of 3" card system to keep progression readable while still allowing variation between runs. I extended the player's fire ability set from 2 upgrade tiers to 5 upgrade tiers, covering 7 active fire abilities and 2 passive fire abilities.

I also implemented elemental ability sets for lightning, earth, and wind. Each element includes 3 unique abilities or augments, creating 9 unique elemental abilities total, with 2-3 upgrade tiers per ability.

Elemental Altars

I implemented elemental altars as an in-run reward mechanic for receiving elemental abilities. Altars spawn during the run and create a clear objective that pulls the player into riskier movement decisions instead of only kiting enemies around the arena.

To support the mechanic, I added altar spawning logic and a UI indicator that points toward the altar's location, helping the player read the objective without breaking the pace of combat.

Rain Mode Escalation

Rain Mode continues the game's central theme of a "War on Water" by turning the environment itself into part of the escalation. I implemented rain visuals and shader work, splash particle effects, and additional lighting on abilities to make the mode feel more dramatic and visually reactive.

As the player's build grows to include up to 18 abilities, Rain Mode increases challenge through Tier 2 glowing enemies, preserving engagement and difficulty when the player has gained a much stronger toolset.

Environmental Hazards & Area Denial

To further reinforce the game's central theme of escalating conflict against water, I implemented environmental water hazards that appear during specific high-pressure waves, including Rain Mode encounters. These hazards create temporary danger zones that apply damage-over-time and movement slows, forcing the player to reposition more carefully instead of relying purely on damage scaling.

The system added another layer of decision-making to combat by introducing environmental pressure alongside enemy density, helping later stages feel more dynamic and mechanically demanding without simply increasing enemy numbers.

Performance for High Enemy Density

Because the game's readability depends on maintaining pressure without technical slowdown, I implemented custom object pooling for enemies, projectiles, and effects. That reduced runtime instantiation overhead and let the game sustain denser combat scenarios more reliably.

UI, Feedback & VFX Integration

I developed the full gameplay UI flow, including HUD, level-up cards, pause, death, victory states, altar indicators, and objective feedback, then paired that with hit effects, particles, trails, shaders, and lighting work to improve combat readability.

That integration work was important because the game needed to stay legible as enemy counts, elemental abilities, rain visuals, and overlapping effects increased.

Challenge & Problem Solving

A major challenge was preserving game feel and readability under high enemy density. As the run scales up, large numbers of enemies, rain effects, elemental abilities, and overlapping particles can quickly turn into visual noise, especially in a top-down survivor-like where the player needs to read danger, impact, and safe movement space at a glance.

To solve that, I introduced clearer particle and hit feedback, tuned ability visuals to better communicate impact and area coverage, added lighting to improve the appeal and readability of abilities in Rain Mode, and balanced Tier 2 enemies so later waves still felt threatening without overwhelming the screen. The result was a more readable and satisfying late-game combat state, which was one of the most important quality improvements on the project.
